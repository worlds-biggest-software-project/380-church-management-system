# 380 – Church Management System (ChMS)

**Date:** 2026-05-02

---

## 1. Problem Statement

Churches and faith communities operate as complex organisations combining pastoral care, community programmes, facilities management, financial stewardship, and volunteer coordination—often with small administrative teams and tight budgets. Member data sits in spreadsheets, giving records live in accounting software, small group rosters are maintained by group leaders separately, and event sign-ups come in through a mix of paper forms and consumer apps. This fragmentation means pastoral staff lack a unified view of congregational engagement, volunteers are under- or over-committed, and giving statements require manual reconciliation at year end. The core challenge is a unified platform that manages the full congregational lifecycle—from first visit through deepening involvement, giving, group participation, and volunteer service—while remaining accessible to non-technical church administrators.

## 2. Market Landscape

The ChMS market is mature and competitive, with pricing ranging from $9/month for basic platforms to custom enterprise pricing for larger multi-site churches:

- **Breeze ChMS:** widely praised for ease of use with a clean, intuitive dashboard; effective membership and people management; strong word-of-mouth adoption in mid-sized churches
- **Pushpay:** integrated ChMS and giving platform with volunteer scheduling, rotation management, and burnout-prevention features; serves larger congregations and multi-site churches
- **Planning Center:** modular platform allowing churches to adopt individual tools (People, Giving, Groups, Volunteers, Calendar, Check-Ins) independently or as a suite
- **ChMeetings:** flexible cloud-based platform supporting event coordination, member records, and multi-device access; strong in international markets
- **Elvanto (Digio):** comprehensive ChMS covering services, rosters, groups, and giving; popular in Australia and New Zealand
- **Subsplash:** mobile-first platform with integrated app, media, giving, and group management; favoured by churches with strong digital engagement strategies
- **FellowshipOne / Ministry Platform:** enterprise-tier platforms for large, complex congregations with multiple campuses and ministry departments
- **ChurchTrac:** entry-level option at the lower end of the pricing spectrum

## 3. Key Features & Capabilities

A full-featured church management system consolidates:

- **Membership and people management:** complete household and individual profiles; relationship mapping (families, small group connections); attendance history; group involvement; custom fields for pastoral notes and milestones (baptism, membership class completion)
- **Giving and financial management:** one-time and recurring online giving; multiple giving methods (card, ACH, text-to-give, kiosk); fund allocation; contribution statements; integration with accounting software (QuickBooks, Xero)
- **Small groups and ministry teams:** group directory and roster management; attendance tracking; communication tools for group leaders; curriculum or study material tracking
- **Volunteer coordination:** serving position management; rotation scheduling; availability tracking; request and confirmation workflows; burnout monitoring; household scheduling to coordinate family members on the same service days
- **Events management:** event creation, promotion, RSVP and registration, capacity management, check-in, and post-event reporting; integration with the church calendar
- **Children's and family check-in:** secure check-in and label printing for children's programmes; allergy and custody alert management; volunteer-to-child ratio compliance
- **Communications:** bulk email and SMS to filtered membership segments; automated follow-up sequences for first-time visitors; push notifications via church mobile app
- **Reporting and dashboards:** attendance trends, giving trends, group health metrics, volunteer coverage, and new-visitor follow-up tracking

---

## 4. Technical Considerations

ChMS platforms serve a buyer audience with low technical sophistication and high sensitivity to cost, which drives distinctive design priorities:

- **Ease of use as a primary requirement:** church administrators are often part-time or volunteer staff; steep learning curves lead to platform abandonment; UX simplicity is a genuine competitive differentiator, not just marketing language
- **Mobile-first for congregation members:** giving, group participation, event sign-up, and service rosters must be accessible via polished native or PWA mobile apps; church members increasingly manage all engagement from their phones
- **Data privacy for pastoral records:** pastoral notes, counselling references, and sensitive life-event data require strict access controls and GDPR/CCPA compliance in jurisdictions where these laws apply
- **Financial integrity:** giving records underpin tax-deductible contribution statements (IRS Form 990 in the US); accurate fund accounting, audit trails, and bank reconciliation are legal requirements, not optional features
- **Multi-site architecture:** growing churches add campuses; the platform must support shared directories and consolidated giving reporting while maintaining campus-level visibility for local staff
- **Integration ecosystem:** payroll (for paid staff), accounting (QuickBooks), streaming (YouTube, Vimeo), and communications (Mailchimp, Twilio) integrations are routinely expected; open APIs or Zapier connectivity reduce friction for smaller churches without IT staff
